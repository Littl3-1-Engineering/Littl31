It notices things before you ask.

Most "smart home" software is a stranger living in your house — competent, forgettable, indifferent to the particulars of you. Alfr3d isn't interested in being competent. It's interested in you: the drive that always takes longer than the map says, the lamp that stays on after the last person's left, the version of Tuesday that only looks like Tuesday from the outside. It learns the shape of an ordinary day in this house well enough to notice, quietly, the one moment that isn't — and it says so once, instead of piling on a wall of alerts. A few of the places that shows up:

  • Leaves you enough time to get there — a leave-by time computed from your own calendar and a routing engine that runs on your own hardware, not a Google API key.
  • Notices when the house is empty and something's still on — a light, a space heater, whatever got left running after everyone's gone.
  • Knows when today isn't like your other Tuesdays — first activity two hours late, half the household still home at noon, whatever's actually unusual for you.
  • Picks up where you left off — pause a track on one screen, find it waiting on the next.
  • Goes quiet before bed — no idle chatter in the last stretch before your own Bedtime routine.

None of it is a spec sheet, and none of it is behind a subscription — it's just what Alfr3d Core already does today, free, forever, self-hosted, quietly getting to know the house it lives in.

Home Assistant made self-hosting mainstream. Nabu Casa made it convenient. Alfr3d is built the same way — a real platform running on hardware you own, with a hosted layer you can opt into, never the other way around.

No ad tier. Not now, not ever. That's not a pricing decision, it's a policy.

It behaves like a butler, not a control panel — situational awareness, a personality layer, dashboards built to live on a wall, not hide in an app. The parts of "smart home" that usually stay locked behind someone else's cloud.

/ ALFR3D CORE

ALFR3D Core

“The engine room. I keep it running so you never have to think about it.”

The Nexus dashboard — residents, situational awareness, and live container health, all on one wall panel.

The backend: containerized microservices — API, User, Device, Environment, and Speak — each one independent, coordinating only over Kafka, plus a Python daemon that handles situational awareness and voice routing. The result is a dashboard that actually looks like something you'd want on a wall panel — not a spreadsheet.

Stays free forever, stays self-hosted, stays local-first, no feature ceiling. This is the platform, not the product we're selling — think of it the way Home Assistant thinks of Home Assistant Core.

/ ALFR3D UPLINK

Alfr3d Uplink

“I remember your errands, your appointments, and exactly how you like your coffee. That's the uplink talking.”

Coming soon — in development

Not yet built. The plan: one subscription bundling Relay's remote access, plus push notifications, hosted encrypted backups, and the personality/quips layer that makes ALFR3D feel less like a control panel and more like staff.

Relay — a thin, dumb-pipe connection for remote access with no port-forwarding and no plaintext ever passing through our servers — isn't sold on its own. It's included in Uplink, and included free for the life of any Alfr3d Kit.

Fair-use LLM usage will be included — generous, capped to keep it sustainable, not throttled to nudge you into a bigger plan. Priority support included.
