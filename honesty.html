We tell you what we killed, too.

Most "AI butler" claims in this category are vaporware. Alfr3d's situational-awareness engine is a two-layer learning system running in production — plus the pieces that were investigated and deliberately stopped, rejected or only partly shipped, on real evidence, published here before anyone had to ask.

This isn't a compliance page. It's the cheapest, most honest proof of the ownership thesis we have — a system that tells you what it can't do yet is more trustworthy than one that quietly fakes it.

PART SHIPPED · SA-9

Room-level sensors from ESPHome

The plan: extend situational awareness into per-room presence and environment data from ESPHome nodes on your network. The integration itself is fully built — discovery, schema, API routes, frontend, and push-based state — and it has been verified end to end against a real ESPHome temperature and humidity sensor on the household's own network.

What shipped from it: two indoor-climate cards. One is a fixed-threshold comfort advisory. The other learns what's normal for each time of day in your house and speaks up only when the room genuinely deviates from it.

What didn't: room-level occupancy, the original goal. That needs hardware that can actually sense presence in a room, and the one sensor on the network is a thermometer. Rather than guess at occupancy from a temperature curve, that part stays parked.

Re-entry: the moment a real presence sensor is reachable on the LAN, occupancy picks back up — the plumbing is already there.

KILLED · SA-8

Room-level presence via Bluetooth

The plan: detect which room someone's actually in over BLE, without a camera or microphone in the room — the privacy-respecting way to do presence.

Why it's dead — the household's real Bluetooth adapter is genuinely capable. Two real passive scans found zero stably-identifiable personal devices. Modern phones randomize their BLE MAC address specifically to prevent this kind of tracking — a feature, not a bug, and one Alfr3d isn't going to fight.

Re-entry: not without new hardware that solves identity a different way. It isn't on the roadmap absent that.

STOPPED · SA-12

Predicting what happens next

The plan: move from noticing what's unusual today to actually anticipating what happens next — conditional patterns mined from your own household's history, not a model trained on anyone else's.

Why it's stopped — we first thought it was only a matter of waiting for history. We checked again on 2026-10-31 with about two months of real events, around 15,000 of them structured, and the volume is no longer the problem. The pairs that stand out statistically are one thing's own lifecycle, like a track starting and then stopping, or the same person messaging and then calling. The sequences a household actually cares about, like arriving and then lights or music, or a routine and then a device, have no signal or nothing producing the events. Any window or threshold we picked now would be invented, and a prediction built on invented numbers is just a guess with a confident face.

Re-entry: when device events show up on at least three weeks of ordinary days across at least three devices, or a scheduled routine starts controlling a device, or the household regularly uses another controllable thing at a set time. Until one of those happens there is nothing new to measure.

STOPPED · LEARNING FROM OUTCOMES

Guessing whether you acted on a card

The plan: work out for ourselves whether a card was useful by watching what happened afterwards. A card says the heater is on in an empty house, the heater goes off, so it was useful.

Why it's stopped — we checked eight weeks of real production history on 2026-11-14 and there is almost nothing to link. The one card about an empty house with something left on fired once. The 190 times Alfr3d flagged a broken routine, every subject was a phone, gateway or console, and none of them is something a person could switch off from Alfr3d. Ten leave-by cards were followed by someone leaving zero times. The music card matched 2 of 33 and was confounded anyway, because the card is built from that same playback. Rather than invent a link, we built none.

Re-entry: when the empty-house card has fired about ten times, or the command log holds eight weeks of history (around the end of December 2026), or a card exists whose subject is a device you can control. The honest route is the explicit one: telling Alfr3d got it, dismiss or not useful on a card, which is already in progress.

REJECTED · ALFR3D DECK

Access to every file on your phone

The plan: Deck's built-in File Explorer would browse, copy, move and delete files anywhere on the phone, using Android's all-files access permission.

Why it's gone — Google reviewed that permission for the Play Store release in October 2026 and rejected it. A file manager isn't a launcher's core purpose, and we agreed. Blanket access to everything on a phone is a lot to ask for a side feature. We removed the permission and rebuilt the explorer on Android's own folder picker. It now sees only the folders you choose, and you can remove a folder again at any time.

The cost is real: the explorer is less capable than a full file manager and can't wander the whole device. We took the smaller permission and the weaker feature.

Re-entry: not planned. If we ever want a full file manager, it should be a separate app with its own review, not a permission on your home screen.

STANDING RULES

What governs everything that does ship

No fabricated data. If a card can't be computed from something real, it doesn't render — silence over a fake reading, always.

Nothing here actuates anything. Every card in the situational-awareness engine is informational. None of it flips a switch on its own.

Learned state is inspectable and resettable. A system that learns about your household has to be one you can correct or wipe, not a black box.

Observation, never concern. Cards note that something's unusual — they don't ask if you're alright. That's a promise this product can't keep, so it doesn't make it.

Household-local, always. No cross-household aggregation, no upload, no training on your data. What Alfr3d learns about your house stays on your Kit.
